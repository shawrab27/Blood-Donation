The Science of Blood

Understanding the vital fluid that sustains life, from its cellular components to its intricate journey through the body.

Blood Components

radio_button_checked

Red Blood Cells

Also known as erythrocytes, these cells carry oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body and return carbon dioxide to the lungs to be exhaled.

coronavirus

White Blood Cells

Leukocytes are the body's primary defense against infection and disease. They are part of the immune system, constantly searching for invaders.

scatter_plot

Platelets

Thrombocytes are tiny cell fragments that rush to the site of an injury to form a clot, preventing excessive bleeding.

water

Plasma

The yellowish liquid component of blood that holds the blood cells in whole blood in suspension. It constitutes about 55% of the body's total blood volume.

Hemoglobin

Hemoglobin is an iron-rich protein present in red blood cells that gives blood its red color. It is responsible for carrying oxygen from the lungs to the tissues and organs.

Anemia

A condition where you lack enough healthy red blood cells or hemoglobin to carry adequate oxygen to your body's tissues. It can make you feel tired and weak.

Increasing Hemoglobin: A diet rich in iron (meat, beans, leafy greens), vitamin C (to aid absorption), and folate can help maintain healthy hemoglobin levels.

Blood Circulation

The circulatory system, powered by the heart, is a continuous loop that delivers essential nutrients and oxygen to every cell while removing waste products.

  • Oxygen Delivery: Oxygen-rich blood is pumped from the heart's left side through arteries to tissues.
  • Deoxygenated Return: Veins carry oxygen-poor blood back to the heart's right side.
  • Oxygen Uptake: Blood is sent to the lungs to release carbon dioxide and absorb fresh oxygen.

Blood Clotting

Coagulation, or clotting, is a complex process by which blood changes from a liquid to a gel, forming a blood clot. It potentially results in hemostasis, the cessation of blood loss from a damaged vessel, followed by repair.

healing

Platelets are the first responders to a damaged vessel. They adhere to the site and release chemicals that attract more platelets, forming a plug. Proteins in the plasma then create a fibrin mesh to solidify the clot.
